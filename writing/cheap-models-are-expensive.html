Token price is a trap: pricing AI routing by total cost

Comparing models by price per million tokens distorts architecture decisions in both directions: cheap models hide rework costs, while all-frontier routing overpays on narrow traffic.

Ugur Tuna · 22 November 2026 · 4 minute read

In almost every AI rollout, someone opens the cloud bill and asks one of two extremes: “Why not swap to a tiny cheap model?” or “Why not send everything to our strongest frontier API?”

Both shortcuts fail because token rate cards ignore 85% to 99% of real operating expense. On judgement-heavy tasks, a weak model triggers line-by-line human re-audits that swamp API savings. On narrow, structured classification, running 100% frontier burns budget with zero quality gain. Sound architecture prices inference + human verification + downstream rework together, behind strict data-residency gates.

Small failure rates compound—and weak models rarely hedge

A 5% error rate sounds tolerable in one-shot chat. Across automated workflows that plan, retrieve, invoke tools, and draft across ten to fifty steps, step errors multiply fast [3]:

  • At 99% reliability per step, 90.4% of ten-step runs and roughly 60.5% of fifty-step runs finish cleanly.
  • At 95% per step, 59.9% survive ten steps—and only 7.7% complete fifty steps without a failure.

Lower accuracy would be easy to route around if smaller models flagged uncertainty reliably. Instead, they fail with total confidence. In an ICML 2026 study of fifteen models across two years, Stephan Rabanser, Sayash Kapoor, Arvind Narayanan, and colleagues tested smaller “efficient” models such as GPT-4o mini and Claude Haiku [1]. Calibration error reached 0.54 to 0.67, versus 0.07 to 0.11 for frontier models such as Claude Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.5. On separating right answers from wrong ones, cheap models scored below chance (0.36 to 0.42 AUROC against 0.70 to 0.72). Across all models, financial errors—wrong charges or refunds—were the commonest safety failure.

The checking bill nobody puts on the slide

Once a reviewer finds one invented figure or dropped clause, trust in the rest of the draft vanishes. They cannot patch one line; they must re-check every source. Razeen Rasheed and colleagues documented a sharp example in February 2026 [2]. Auditing an autonomous research agent, evaluators found a fluent paper claiming training efficiency gains when its code actually burned 23% more compute and 18% more wall-clock time—leaving human experts hours of log debugging.

Pricing three routing regimes properly

Because neither “buy cheap everywhere” nor “run frontier everywhere” works across workloads, ai-systems-architecture evaluates residency gates alongside full monthly TCO. The table below compares synthetic assumptions across three bundled blueprints (illustrative planning parameters, not live vendor quotes):

Synthetic regime Routing option Inference / mo Review + rework / mo Total / mo (status)
Sovereign briefing (25k/mo) Public API vs Sovereign VPC frontier vs Self-hosted 70B £450 vs £4,790 vs £10,237 £46,958 vs £46,958 vs £72,698 Public blocked; VPC £51,749 wins vs £82,934

Financial risk triage (400k/mo) All-frontier vs 85/15 open-weights-to-frontier cascade £6,260 vs £3,409 £127,000 vs £128,530 £133,260 vs £131,939 (cascade wins above 254k/mo)

Three practical lessons stand out across those regimes:

  • Residency beats token price first: in the sovereign briefing workload, a £450/month public API fails sovereignty gates outright. Meanwhile, an error-prone self-hosted 70B cluster adds £25,740/month in rework, leaving Sovereign VPC frontier ahead at 9.3% inference vs 90.7% human effort.
  • Unverified small models destroy ROI: in the evidence copilot regime, a £27/month small model with a 2.0% error rate triggers £65,719/month in review and rework—a £23,746/month net loss. Pre-routing complex 70% queries to grounded frontier restores positive net savings.
  • Evaluation-gated cascades beat extremes once volume clears break-even: in financial triage, break-even sits at 254,073 requests/month; at 400k/month, routing routine 85% cases to open weights and cascading 15% cuts inference spend by 45.5% (£3,409 vs £6,260) while lowering total cost (£131,939 vs £133,260). On narrow high-volume extraction where evals confirm parity and throughput amortises dedicated GPU and standby capacity, self-hosted open weights can win on both cost and latency (480 ms vs 1,350 ms p95).

Four rules for model routing

  1. Enforce residency and retention gates before pricing tokens: disqualify non-compliant endpoints upfront.
  2. Model total cost, never just API bills: include reviewer minutes and downstream error cost alongside inference.
  3. Choose model mixes on evaluation evidence: promote open-weights tiers or cascades only after fixed-set testing proves parity.
  4. Keep human backstops targeted: attach source links and confidence bounds so reviewers verify risky items rather than re-reading everything [1].
