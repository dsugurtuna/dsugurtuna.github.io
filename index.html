How I lead

  • Activity is not impact.

    Usage counts show exposure, not whether outputs were accurate or decisions improved.

  • Price total human effort.

    Verification, review, and downstream rework belong inside the true ROI of every AI workflow.

  • A second model is a critic, not a verifier.

    Models share blind spots; agreement between models is not ground truth.

  • Permission to build is not permission to deploy.

    Connecting data, exposing tools, and authorising autonomous action are distinct risk gates.

  • Solve the data problem before adding an agent.

    Deterministic automation or cleaner structure beats fragile autonomy whenever possible.

  • Surface uncertainty explicitly.

    Systems that expose what is unverified earn trust; systems that hide gaps fail silently.

Writing

Essays on working well with AI, frontier economics, multi-model teams, evaluation, and runtime control.

  • Write your own prompts: why borrowed libraries hollow out judgement

    A prompt library hands you fluent output while quietly taking away the only part of the work that teaches you whether it is any good.

  • A partner, not a button: why full automation turns you into the AI’s servant

    Good AI work grows out of a working relationship built over time. Remove the conversation and you are left clicking approve on decisions you no longer own.

  • Cheap models are expensive: why frontier intelligence and human eyes win

    Agent frameworks love small, fast models because the API bill looks tidy. The real cost shows up later in hallucinations, broken chains and hours of human rework.

  • AI is not a coding tool: why the biggest value sits outside the IDE

    The AI industry talks as though language models exist mainly to write software. Most of the people actually using them are thinking through strategy, learning, law and everyday decisions.

  • Roles from data, not from dice: evidence-gated multi-model teams

    Different AI labs build different minds from different worlds of data. Put them together by measured strength, and force every counter-argument to bring verifiable evidence.

  • Usage is not quality: what to measure when you roll out AI

    Usage counts show who clicked. They do not show whether the work got better, or what it cost to check.

  • A second model is a critic, not a verifier

    Asking one model to review another can improve a draft. It cannot tell you the draft is true.

  • Read freely, write carefully: controls for AI agents that act

    An agent that reads can mislead you. An agent that writes or sends can do something you cannot take back.
